Signs of a Drain or Sewer Problem

Drain and sewer problems range from a nuisance to a health hazard, and the difference between the two can develop quickly. Here are the warning signs that tell you it is time to call a plumber rather than reaching for a plunger:

  • Multiple slow drains. If more than one drain in your home is running slowly, the problem is likely in the main drain or sewer line, not individual fixtures.
  • Sewage odors. A sewer smell inside the home or near the foundation means something is not draining properly, or a vent pipe may be blocked.
  • Gurgling sounds. Gurgling from toilets or drains when you run water elsewhere in the house indicates air in the line, which usually means a blockage downstream.
  • Water backing up. If water comes back up through a floor drain, shower, or tub when you flush a toilet or run a washing machine, you have a main line blockage.
  • Wet spots in the yard. Unexplained soggy areas or unusually green patches of grass near the sewer line can indicate a break or leak in the sewer pipe.

When a Slow Drain Is More Than a Clog

A single slow drain is often a simple clog that can be cleared. But when drains throughout the house start slowing down, or when a cleared drain keeps coming back, the problem is usually deeper than what a plunger or drain snake can reach.

Common causes of recurring or system-wide drain problems include:

  • Tree root intrusion. Roots from nearby trees can enter sewer pipes through cracks or joints. They grow inside the pipe, catching debris and creating progressively worse blockages. This is particularly common in Lincoln County, where many properties have established trees and older clay or concrete sewer pipes.
  • Pipe deterioration. Older sewer lines made of clay, Orangeburg (tar paper), or cast iron deteriorate over time. The pipe walls roughen, bellies develop where the pipe sags, and sections can crack or collapse.
  • Grease accumulation. Grease poured down kitchen drains coats the inside of the pipe and hardens. Over time, this narrows the pipe and catches everything else that flows through. Once a grease blockage is deep enough in the line, it takes professional equipment to clear.
  • Ground movement. The wet Oregon Coast soil shifts and settles, which can cause sewer pipes to develop bellies, separate at joints, or crack. These issues trap waste and create chronic slow drainage.

If you are dealing with a drain problem that keeps coming back, the only way to know what is actually happening inside the pipe is to have it professionally assessed. We can determine whether the issue is a simple blockage or something that requires repair to the pipe itself.

Preventing Drain and Sewer Problems

While some drain and sewer issues are unavoidable, especially with older pipes and tree roots, there are practical steps you can take to reduce the likelihood of problems:

  • Use drain screens. Inexpensive mesh screens over shower and sink drains catch hair and debris before they enter the pipe.
  • Keep grease out of drains. Pour cooking grease into a disposable container and throw it in the trash. Even small amounts of grease accumulate inside pipes over time.
  • Flush only toilet paper. Wipes, paper towels, feminine products, and other items do not break down like toilet paper and cause blockages.
  • Run water with the disposal. When using a garbage disposal, run cold water before, during, and for several seconds after to help flush material through the drain line.
  • Know your sewer line. If you have large trees on your property and an older sewer line, periodic inspection can catch root intrusion before it causes a backup.
