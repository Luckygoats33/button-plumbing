Signs Your Water Heater Needs Attention

A failing water heater does not always announce itself with a dramatic leak. More often, it gives you warning signs over weeks or months. Knowing what to watch for can help you get ahead of the problem before you are stuck without hot water or dealing with water damage.

  • Lukewarm or inconsistent water temperature. If the hot water is not as hot as it used to be, or it runs out faster than normal, the heating element, thermostat, or burner may be failing.
  • Rusty or discolored hot water. Rust-colored water from the hot tap usually means the anode rod is depleted and the tank is beginning to corrode from inside.
  • Popping, rumbling, or banging sounds. Sediment builds up on the bottom of the tank over time. When the burner heats the sediment layer, it causes these noises. This reduces efficiency and accelerates tank deterioration.
  • Water pooling around the base. Any water around the base of the unit could mean a leaking tank, a failing pressure relief valve, or loose connections. A leaking tank cannot be repaired and needs replacement.
  • Age. If your water heater is approaching or past the 10-year mark, it is worth having it inspected even if it seems to be working fine. Problems tend to compound quickly once a unit starts aging out.

Repair vs. Replace: How to Decide

This is one of the most common questions we hear, and the answer depends on a few factors:

  • Age of the unit. If the water heater is less than 8 years old and the problem is a component like a thermostat, heating element, or valve, repair is usually the right move. Past 10 years, replacement starts to make more financial sense.
  • Type of failure. A leaking tank cannot be repaired. If the tank itself is the problem, replacement is the only option. Component failures like thermostats and elements can be repaired.
  • Cost comparison. If a repair costs more than half the price of a new unit, replacement is generally the better investment. You get a new warranty and a fresh start on the unit's lifespan.
  • Frequency of problems. If you have had the unit repaired more than once in the past year, the overall system is likely declining and further repairs will be short-lived.

We do not push replacement when a repair will do the job, and we do not patch a unit that should be replaced. You get a straight answer.

Types of Water Heaters

Button Plumbing works on all common residential water heater types. Here is a quick overview of what is available and what each type is best suited for:

  • Standard tank (gas). The most common type in Lincoln County homes. Heats and stores water in an insulated tank. Reliable and relatively affordable. Typical sizes range from 40 to 50 gallons for a household.
  • Standard tank (electric). Similar to gas but uses electric heating elements. Common in homes without natural gas service, which includes many properties in the Siletz area and rural Lincoln County.
  • Tankless (on-demand). Heats water as it flows through the unit rather than storing it. Uses less energy when demand is low, but installation costs are higher. Good for homes where space is limited or hot water demand is modest.

The right type depends on your home's existing infrastructure, your hot water usage patterns, and your budget. We can walk you through the options and help you choose what makes the most sense for your situation.

Water Heater Maintenance Matters

A water heater is one of those appliances most people ignore until it stops working. A little maintenance goes a long way toward extending its life and keeping it efficient:

  • Annual flushing removes sediment that builds up on the bottom of the tank. Sediment reduces heating efficiency and shortens the tank's life.
  • Anode rod inspection every two to three years. The anode rod protects the tank from corrosion. Once it is depleted, the tank starts corroding. Replacing a spent rod is far cheaper than replacing the entire water heater.
  • Temperature and pressure relief valve testing ensures this critical safety device is working properly.

If you cannot remember the last time your water heater was serviced, it is probably overdue. Call us to schedule a maintenance visit.
