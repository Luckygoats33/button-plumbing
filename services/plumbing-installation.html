New Construction Plumbing

If you are building a new home or commercial building in Lincoln County, the plumbing is one of the most critical systems to get right. Mistakes during installation are expensive to fix once the walls are closed up. Here is what new construction plumbing involves:

  • Underground rough-in. Before the slab is poured or the floor is laid, the sewer lines and water supply lines that run under the building are installed. This phase sets the locations for toilets, showers, and other fixtures.
  • Top-out rough-in. After framing, the supply and drain lines are run through walls and floors to each fixture location. Vent pipes are run through the roof. This work has to pass inspection before drywall goes up.
  • Fixture installation. After finishing work is complete, the fixtures themselves are installed: sinks, faucets, toilets, tubs, showers, and appliance connections.
  • Testing and inspection. The complete system is tested for leaks, proper drainage, and code compliance before the project is signed off.

We coordinate with general contractors to keep the project on schedule and make sure the plumbing is done right at each phase.

Fixture Upgrades and Replacements

Upgrading plumbing fixtures is one of the most practical improvements you can make to a home. New fixtures can improve water efficiency, eliminate ongoing drip and leak problems, and update the look of a kitchen or bathroom without a full remodel.

Common fixture upgrades include:

  • Kitchen faucets. Modern kitchen faucets offer better functionality, including pull-down sprayers and single-handle operation. Replacing an old, leaky faucet saves water and improves daily use.
  • Toilets. Newer toilets use significantly less water per flush than models from previous decades. If you have a toilet that runs, clogs frequently, or uses more water than necessary, replacement is worth considering.
  • Bathroom faucets and showerheads. Updated fixtures can improve water pressure, reduce waste, and eliminate persistent drip problems.
  • Shut-off valves. Old gate-style shut-off valves often freeze in place and fail when you need them most. Replacing them with modern quarter-turn ball valves is a smart upgrade.

When we install a new fixture, we also inspect the shut-off valves, supply lines, and drain connections. If anything needs attention, we let you know. It is much easier to address these items when the area is already being worked on.

What Is Involved in a Plumbing Installation

Every installation project is different, but the general process follows a predictable pattern:

  • Assessment. We look at the existing plumbing, confirm what modifications are needed, and discuss the scope of work with you.
  • Planning. For anything beyond a simple fixture swap, we plan the routing of supply and drain lines, check code requirements, and coordinate with other trades if needed.
  • Installation. We install the plumbing, connect to the existing system, and test everything for leaks and proper operation.
  • Cleanup and walkthrough. We clean up the work area and walk you through what was done. If there is anything you should know about maintaining the new installation, we will tell you.
