Planning Your Remodel: The Plumbing Perspective

If you are planning a kitchen or bathroom remodel, here is what to think about from a plumbing standpoint:

  • Get the plumber involved early. Before you finalize your layout, talk to a plumber about fixture placement. Some locations are straightforward to plumb; others require significant rerouting of supply and drain lines. Knowing this upfront helps you make informed decisions about your design.
  • Understand what is in the walls. Older homes in Lincoln County may have galvanized steel pipes, cast iron drain lines, or plumbing that does not meet current codes. A remodel is the ideal time to update these systems since the walls are already open.
  • Plan for venting. Every drain needs a vent to function properly. When fixtures are moved, the venting may need to be reconfigured. This is one of the most commonly overlooked aspects of remodel plumbing.
  • Consider the water heater. If you are adding a bathroom or upgrading to a larger tub or shower, make sure your water heater can handle the increased demand. A remodel is a good time to evaluate whether the water heater needs attention.
  • Budget for the unexpected. When walls come open in older homes, you sometimes find plumbing that was done incorrectly, pipes that are deteriorating, or conditions that need to be addressed before the new work can proceed. A realistic budget includes some room for this.

The Rough-In Process

Rough-in is the phase of a remodel where the plumbing goes inside the walls and floors before they are closed up. It is the foundation that everything else is built on. Here is what happens during a typical rough-in:

  • Demolition and assessment. Once the old fixtures and finishes are removed, we assess the existing plumbing. We identify what can be reused, what needs to be replaced, and what modifications are required for the new layout.
  • Pipe routing. We run new supply and drain lines to the planned fixture locations. This includes cutting and fitting pipe through framing, connecting to the existing system, and installing proper venting.
  • Inspection. The rough-in must pass a plumbing inspection before the walls and floors can be closed up. The inspector verifies that the work meets Oregon plumbing codes, the pipes are properly supported, and the venting is correct.
  • Finish phase. After drywall, tile, and finishing work are complete, we return to install the fixtures: sinks, faucets, toilets, tubs, showerheads, and appliance connections.

Working With Your Contractor

If you are working with a general contractor on your remodel, we coordinate directly with them to keep the project on schedule. The plumber needs to be on site at specific points in the construction timeline, and good coordination prevents delays.

If you are managing the remodel yourself, we can help you understand the plumbing phases and what order things need to happen. The key milestones are:

  • Plumber consultation before finalizing the layout
  • Rough-in after demolition and framing, before insulation and drywall
  • Inspection after rough-in, before closing walls
  • Fixture installation after painting and tile are complete

Getting these phases in the right order saves time and prevents rework. We have been through this process on many remodel projects in Lincoln County and can help you plan accordingly.
